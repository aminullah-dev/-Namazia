# Azan recordings

Both apps play these, from `app/src/main/res/raw/`. The iOS notification sounds in
`ios/Namazia/Resources/Sounds/` are cut from the same two files.

## Regular azan — `azan.mp3`, `azan30.caf`

- **Recording:** "Beautiful adhan", by Adam-synagda
- **Source:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Beautiful_adhan.ogg
- **License:** CC0 1.0 — public domain dedication, no conditions
  (https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/)
- **Changes:** Wikimedia's MP3 transcode of the original Ogg, unaltered.
  `azan30.caf` is its first 29.35 seconds, mono.

## Fajr azan — `azan_fajr.mp3`, `azan_fajr30.caf`

- **Recording:** "Morning call to prayer on Bodufolhudhoo, Maldives", by jrosin
- **Source:** https://freesound.org/people/jrosin/sounds/861625/
- **License:** CC BY 4.0 — free to use, including commercially, with credit
  (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)
- **Changes:** trimmed to 4.5–273.5 s of the original, removing silence at both ends.
  `azan_fajr30.caf` is 1.5–29.5 s of that, mono.

The credit CC BY asks for is in the iOS Settings footer (`settings.about.footer`)
and in both store descriptions. Keep it there as long as this recording ships.

## Why these

The recordings they replaced had no known source, which left the App Store's content
rights question unanswerable. Both of these are Sunni: twelve phrases in the regular
azan, fourteen in Fajr with «الصلاة خير من النوم», none of the Shia additions.
